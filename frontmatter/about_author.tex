	\textbf{Mahdi} (\href{https://github.com/m-mdy-m}{@m-mdy-m}) is a self-taught backend
	developer and open-source author based in Iran. His interest in computing started not
	from a classroom but from a recurring sense of dissatisfaction: tutorials and courses
	kept telling him \emph{how} to use a tool---an array, a hash table, a CPU instruction---without
	ever explaining \emph{why} it worked the way it did, or what was actually happening
	underneath.\\[0.5em]
	That dissatisfaction eventually turned into a habit of tracing every concept back to its
	roots: from a programming construct to its memory layout, from memory layout to the
	hardware that implements it, and from the hardware to the physics underneath that. Arliz
	is the written record of that habit, applied specifically to the array---arguably the
	single most important data structure in all of computing, and one of the most
	under-explained.\\[0.5em]
	Alongside Arliz, he maintains and continues to develop two companion works: \textit{Mathesis:
	The Mathematical Foundations of Computing} and \textit{The Art of Algorithmic Analysis},
	both written for the same reason---to provide the depth that introductory material
	usually skips.
\end{minipage}
